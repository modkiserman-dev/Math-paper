\section{Toward \texorpdfstring{$D(x)$}{D(x)}: outlook}\label{sec:outlook}
The identity~\eqref{eq:main-identity} expresses $D(x)$ exactly in
terms of the prime-power contribution and the alternating finite sum
of the quantities $T_j(x)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, however, is a
fixed-$j$ asymptotic statement. Since the range of relevant $j$ grows
with $x$, these fixed-$j$ asymptotics cannot be inserted term by term
into~\eqref{eq:main-identity} to obtain an asymptotic formula for
$D(x)$.

Equivalently, with
\[
A(x,y)=\sum_{j\geq0}T_j(x)y^j,
\]
the alternating sum satisfies
\[
    \sum_{j\geq2}(-1)^j(j-1)T_j(x)
    = A(x,0)-A(x,-1)-A_y(x,-1).
\]
Here $A(x,-1)=1$ and $A_y(x,-1)=\sum_{i\geq1}(i+1)\pi(x^{1/i})$
account for the $n=1$ and prime-power contributions, while the full
alternating sum also involves $A(x,0)=D(x)$ itself. The analytic
work in this paper is carried out for $y$ in a small neighborhood of
$0$. Extending the method toward $D(x)$ would therefore require
estimates uniform in $j$, or a direct analysis of the generating
function near $y=-1$.
